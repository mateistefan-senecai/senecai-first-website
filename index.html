Context

The EU has been building a digital constitution.

In under a decade, the EU has passed the GDPR, NIS2, DORA, the Cyber Resilience Act and the AI Act — an interlocking set of rules that now touches almost every company that builds, sells or uses digital technology in Europe. They weren't designed as one system, but in practice they function like one: obligations overlap, deadlines collide, and a single AI system can trigger duties under four or five of them at once.

The stakes aren't theoretical. AI Act fines reach €35M or 7% of global turnover. GDPR fines reach €20M or 4% of turnover. NIS2 introduces personal liability for management when security governance fails.

That's the case for one governance approach instead of five separate ones — which is exactly what the SenecAI Governance Hub is built to do.

Our Services

AI ACT

AI Act Conformity Assessment

Every AI Act obligation starts with knowing what you're actually running. Most companies don't have a clear picture of every AI system they've built, bought, or quietly embedded through a vendor — and without that picture, you can't determine your role under the Act or what it requires of you.

We build and maintain a living inventory of your AI systems, determine whether you're a provider, deployer, or both under Article 3, and classify each system's risk level against Article 6 and Annex III. For systems that land in scope, we take you through the full conformity assessment pathway: the risk management system and technical documentation the Act requires (Articles 9 and 11), the EU Declaration of Conformity (Article 47), CE marking (Article 48), and registration in the EU database where applicable (Article 49).

What we help you with

  • AI system inventory and provider/deployer determination (Article 3)
  • Risk classification against Article 6 and Annex III
  • Technical documentation, Declaration of Conformity, CE marking and EU database registration (Articles 11, 47–49)
AI ACT

Governing Agentic Workflows

Agentic systems make decisions and take actions with less human oversight at every step — and when those decisions fall within a high-risk use case under Annex III, you inherit the full set of Article 9–15 obligations, not a lighter version of them.

We help you govern agentic workflows against what the Act actually requires: a documented risk management system covering the agent's decision scope (Article 9), logging sufficient to reconstruct and trace what the agent did and why (Article 12), and human oversight measures that let a person actually intervene or shut it down (Article 14) — before a regulator, customer or your own board asks who's accountable when the agent acts.

What we help you with

  • Human oversight design and accountability mapping (Article 14)
  • Logging and record-keeping for automated decisions (Article 12)
  • Risk management system design for agentic components (Article 9)
