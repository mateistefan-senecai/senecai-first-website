What is SenecAI?

SenecAI is a compliance partner for startups and SMEs navigating the EU digital regulatory landscape — the AI Act, GDPR, NIS2, DORA, the Cyber Resilience Act and more. We combine legal, technical and cybersecurity expertise to take you from "what applies to us?" to fully compliant.

Who do you work with?

Mostly startups and SMEs building or using AI, though our services extend to any company facing GDPR, NIS2, DORA or CRA obligations — from early-stage teams preparing for investor due diligence to scale-ups closing enterprise clients.

EU regulations, in plain terms
What is the EU AI Act, and does it apply to us?

The AI Act is the EU's risk-based framework regulating the development, deployment and use of AI systems. Whether — and how — it applies depends on your role (provider, deployer, distributor or importer) and your system's risk classification. Most companies using AI in the EU market carry some obligation, even if it's only transparency requirements. We run inventory and classification exercises to tell you exactly where you stand.

Does GDPR still apply if we're building AI products?

Yes — training data, inference logs and any personal data processed by an AI system are still subject to GDPR. AI doesn't create a carve-out; it usually adds obligations, like DPIAs for higher-risk processing or a clearer legal basis for the data you train on.

What is NIS2 and who does it apply to?

NIS2 is the EU's updated cybersecurity directive. It extends governance and incident-reporting obligations to a much wider set of "essential" and "important" entities than before, and puts personal liability on management for non-compliance. Whether you're in scope is usually the first question we answer in a NIS2 gap assessment.

What is DORA, and does it only apply to banks?

DORA (the Digital Operational Resilience Act) applies to financial entities and their critical ICT providers — so it reaches far beyond banks, into any vendor providing critical ICT services to the financial sector. It requires a documented ICT risk management framework, incident reporting and resilience testing, not just a security policy on paper.

What is the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)?

The CRA sets cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements sold in the EU — hardware and software alike — covering secure-by-design development, vulnerability handling and incident reporting throughout the product's lifecycle.

What happens if we don't comply?

Penalties vary by regulation: the AI Act allows fines up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover, GDPR up to €20M or 4%, and NIS2 puts personal liability on management. Beyond fines, non-compliance increasingly shows up in investor due diligence and enterprise procurement — it's a commercial risk, not just a legal one.

How do I know which regulations actually apply to my business?

That depends on your products, business model, and how you use data and AI — there's no one-size-fits-all answer. Try the compliance checker above, or book a free intro call and we'll map your exposure in a Discover session.
